The Weave of the Visible

Artist

José Antonio Farrera

A curatorial reflection on materiality, pictorial instability and the floral still life as an active surface of construction.

Article Details
Category

Exhibition Essay

Artist

José Antonio Farrera

Medium

Oil on Canvas

In this exhibition, José Antonio Farrera extends his investigation into pictorial materiality into the genre of floral still life. Far removed from any ornamental function, these vases and bouquets operate as fields of experimentation where painting asserts itself as an active surface —a space in which the image does not settle but is continuously constructed and destabilized.

Farrera builds his paintings using the conceptual tools of drawing: linear weaves, hatching, and grid-like patterns that structure the surface. Upon this framework, he applies dense layers of oil paint, only to intervene again—scraping, incising, and sanding.

“Rather than painting flowers, Farrera paints painting itself.”

This back-and-forth produces areas of high material density where form oscillates between definition and dissolution. The flowers thus cease to be closed entities: they vibrate, fragment, and hover at a threshold where the figurative brushes against the abstract, even approaching optical instability.

Here, the “weave”—a warp of reliefs, interferences, and chromatic variations—renders visible what usually remains concealed: the flesh of oil paint, the memory of the brush, and the exuberance of life that exceeds the limits of the canvas.

The viewer, then, does not merely observe, but moves through—almost touches—the intricate weave of the visible.
